Week 7 Codex CLI Hands On Reflection

Beginning with where Codex struggled, looking at the code it produced it seems extremely 'verbose'. While I did tweak a few things I largely left the code that Codex output to itself, and only fixed a few errors I could clearly identify and readily fix. I also opted for a Jupyter Notebook and doing my work in Python, which I expected Codex to handle a bit better than R since I've experienced issues there in the past. Obviously there's limitations surrounding what LLMs are capable of, but the handful of mistakes it made seemed largely 'unhuman'. Some non-unicode characters appeared, I experienced an issue with Java encoding at one point, and also had to basically beat it into submission on the output of my analysis when it came to a simple formatting error. There were also a handful of instances in which it repeatedly asked for permission to perform what I would perceive as menial tasks, but the LLM was surprisingly coy about write permissions. 

I was most surprised, however, at how capable it was of taking a simple input for what I thought would be an intermediary step and running with it to completion. When I instructed it to begin on a rough first draft for my final analysis it took the results and without instruction, tied together the parts that I wanted to comment on such as the limitations of the data, and how I wanted to conclude the analysis/actually answer the question I posed. I was also surprised at how efficiently it was able to pull the outside data I wanted to look at for my analysis. In the past whenever I've tried to build tools to scrape the web it was all manually done and I ran into a number of issues with how the internet general allows bots to access information. I gave Codex one url to pull from and it was able to not only get all of the data I was looking for, but interpret and format it. 

If I were to run the task again I would simply choose a different question with what I know now. After driving through Iowa this summer I wanted to ask a question regarding all of their iconography for tornadoes, to see if they had any impact on buyer preferences. After running through my analysis, the answer was really just a simple 'no', however. While this is an answer by itself, and Codex handled all the steps along the way with relative ease, the relationship between buyer preferences and tornadoes was simply not apparent in such a short timeframe as 2006-10. From a procedural standpoint, however, I think it would've been better for me to chart out my plan from the beginning (and ensure that all of the ai-workflow-logs were being timestamped), so as not to wander aimlessly while I set Codex to perform tasks without a clear direction in mind and just acting on the responses it gave me.
